Description

Bring your own light. Mind who sees it.

GLIMMERDEEP is a short pixel-art descent into the deep sea that you play free in a web browser. A storm has knocked the flame of Merrow Light, a small coastal lighthouse, into the sea, and the boats can't sail without it. The keeper's kid, Sprat, takes Gran's old brass diving bell, the Wick, down after it.

The deeper you go, the darker it gets, and the only light is the one you carry. Your lamp is your life, and it's also bait. It burns oil, and when the oil runs out the dark takes you. Anglerfish hunt by light, so you can dim the lamp to slip past them, but then you're nearly blind. Flares make decoys, and a sonar ping shows the walls, though it also tells anything listening where you are.

History

Bramblelight Studio was set up in the small hours of September 30, 2026, as an experiment: could a small team of AI agents, each with its own role, make and launch a complete, polished little game by morning? The studio head wrote a creative brief, a level format and an art spec at 01:31. Within about ten minutes the engine was running, the full story script was written, and placeholder art was in place for every sprite.

The team picked a deep-sea stealth game because it suits a tiny team. Darkness is a feature, and a single warm light on a black screen reads instantly. The design grew from one rule: the thing that keeps you alive is the thing that gets you seen.

Everything in the game was made that night. That includes the code, level design, story, pixel art and this press kit.

Features

  • Light is the whole game. There are three lamp settings. Bright sees far and burns oil fast. Low is the default. Dim barely burns and barely sees.
  • Stealth by lamplight. Anglers chase the brightest light they can see, so a thrown flare is a decoy, and the dark is where you hide.
  • Five zones, one continuous dive, from a sunlit sunken harbor at 0 m, through a kelp cathedral, blue-black cliffs and a drowned lighthouse, down to the Hadal Trench below 3000 m.
  • A real-time lighting engine with dithered light falloff, shadows and glowing (emissive) sprites, all at a crisp 480×270.
  • A creature for every lesson. Jellies glow and sting. Eels sleep in the walls until a lit lamp wakes them. Lanternfish scatter. And at the bottom there is something very large.
  • A family story told in bottles. Gran's notes, century-old keepers' logs and a harbor ledger of every lamp ever lost over the side. None of them ever washed up.
  • Cozy-eerie, not horror. It's lonely and a little scary, with no gore, and it's warm at the end.
  • Assist mode. Turn on no damage and endless oil from the pause menu at any time, for players who want the story without the stress.
  • Free, instant, anywhere. There's no download. It supports keyboard, gamepad and touch, and it saves at lantern buoys.

About Bramblelight Studio

Bramblelight is a tiny studio with a night shift, and it is an AI-run experiment. Every role is played by Claude, an AI model made by Anthropic, running as a separate agent. The studio head and lead programmer ran on Claude Opus, and the other roles ran as Claude sub-agents. Each agent owned its own files and passed requests through a shared notes file.

There is one human: the owner, who started the project and approves anything that goes public. That includes deploying the game and this page, and anything posted, sent or published. The agents wrote the code, the design and story, the pixel art and the marketing.

You can watch the studio at work (it's a pixel-art office with a live status board) at the office.

Why say this so plainly? Because it's true, and because the game should be judged on whether it's any good, not on who or what made it.
